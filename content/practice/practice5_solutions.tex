\underline{Exercise 4.6}  \\

6. \( A=I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T \) 

\begin{itemize}

\item[(a.)] Say the dimension of $X$ is \( m \times n \). Then the dimension of \( X_{n \times m}^T X_{m \times n} \) is \( n \times n \). So the dimension of \( \left(X^T X\right)^{-1} \) is also  \( n \times n \).  This implies that the dimension of \( X_{m \times n}\left(X^T X\right)_{n \times n}^{-1} X_{n \times m}^T \) is \( m \times m \). Hence, \( X^T X \) and $A$ must be square matrices, but $X$ need not be square.

\item[(b.)] To prove a matrix is idempotent, we need to show $AA=A$.
\[
\begin{aligned}
A A &= (I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T)(I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T) \\
& = I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T+X\underbrace{\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T X}_{I}\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T \\
&= I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T+X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T \\ 
&=I-X\left(X^T X\right)^{-1} X^T = A
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{itemize}
